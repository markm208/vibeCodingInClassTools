How it works

The blue bar shows what happens next. Each press of Space (or Next Step) performs exactly one thing: one hardware event, or one scheduler action in response. When P1 blocks on I/O, that is one step; the short term scheduler dispatching P2 is the next step. Leave the dropdown on Random to go with the flow, or pick a specific event to set up your own experiment. Scheduler reactions cannot be chosen; the schedulers must respond to what happened. Predict the result before each press. Hardware events include: a new process is created, a timer interrupt fires, an I/O device finishes, a running process requests I/O, finishes its work, or performs an illegal operation. The three schedulers then react.

long term decides when a new process is admitted to the ready state. A new process only holds its PCB (stored in the OS's kernel memory) until admission. If RAM is full, admission is delayed.

short term dispatches a ready process to the CPU and handles timeouts, I/O requests, and I/O completions.

medium term swaps blocked process images out to disk when memory is tight, and swaps ready/suspended images back into RAM when room opens up.

hardware entries are interrupts and other events that the schedulers respond to.

Every step gets a time marker (t1, t2, t3, ...) and is saved to the timeline at the bottom. Use the left and right arrow keys to walk backward and forward through history one step at a time. You can also hover over the slider to peek at any time step, drag it, or click a tick mark. The Activity Log holds the full record.
